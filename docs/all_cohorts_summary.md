## 2. Accuracy per model

With prediction $\hat y$ and age $y$ over the $N$ units of a cohort, $\bar y$ its mean age:

$$ \mathrm{MAE} = \frac{1}{N}\sum \lvert \hat y - y \rvert, \qquad \mathrm{bias} = \frac{1}{N}\sum (\hat y - y), \qquad R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum (\hat y - y)^2}{\sum (y - \bar y)^2} \tag{2.1} $$

Intervals are 95 % percentile intervals over 2,000 bootstrap resamples of people.

| AHEPA | no | device-robust E2L, 10.69 | controls predicted about 10 y too young, cause unknown; patient / control contrast only |
| Iowa Parkinson's | no | device-robust E2L, 10.09 | controls predicted about 11 y too young, cause unknown; patient / control contrast only |
| Tel Aviv Parkinson's | no | device-robust E2L, 7.89 | controls predicted about 7 y too young, cause unknown; patient / control contrast only |
| MIPDB adults | no | P3 ensemble, 6.88 | 31 recordings: too few; left out of final reports |

**What the tables show:**
- **On the 4 external cohorts that resemble the training data** (TDBRAIN, LEMON, Dortmund Vital Study, SRM) the P4 ensemble reaches 4.53 to 4.92 years zero-shot, with $R^2$ 0.79 to 0.90: at or below the error on the held-out Temple and Harvard patients.
- **The ensembles beat the window model E2L on every cohort where the models work;** P4 and P3 are within 0.3 years of each other there.
- **The device-robust model is better than E2L only on the cohorts where the raw-input models fail** (CHBP, NMT, AHEPA, Iowa Parkinson's, Tel Aviv Parkinson's), and worse everywhere else, by 0.3 to 3.0 years.
- **Five cohorts are not age benchmarks for the present models:** NMT (released band-limited), the three elderly disease cohorts (their controls are predicted 7 to 11 years too young) and the MIPDB adults (31 recordings). They serve for contrasts inside a cohort, or not at all.
